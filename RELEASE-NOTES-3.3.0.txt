JRL Messages 3.3.0 - iPhone notifications over Bluetooth, no Phone Link

What this is
Your iPhone hands its notifications to Bluetooth devices paired directly
to it; that is how a smartwatch shows them and how Phone Link works.
JRL Messages now speaks that protocol itself (Apple's Notification
Center Service), so your iPhone's app notifications (social, email,
banking, calendar, calls) appear as this app's own popups, marked
"· iPhone", with the same sound, in your tint, at your text size, and
recorded in the bell. No Phone Link needed.

How it behaves
- Obeys your existing switches: Popups and Sound apply exactly as they
  do for messages; sound-only mode works; everything lands in the bell.
- Texts are never mirrored, deliberately. They already arrive through
  the Mac with full history and richer popups, so one message can never
  alert you twice.
- A Mute apps box silences noisy apps by name (e.g. "instagram").
- The link reconnects by itself with patient backoff when the phone
  leaves and re-enters range, and narrates everything in the Activity
  panel and the bell: connected, lost, retrying, pairing.
- Bursts are storm-safe: the same circuit breaker that guards message
  floods bounds mirrored floods, and redelivered notifications never
  duplicate in the bell.

Setup, once (about two minutes)
1. Pair the iPhone with this PC: Windows Settings > Bluetooth & devices
   > Add device > pick your iPhone, confirm the code on both screens.
2. In the app: Settings > Alerts > turn on "Mirror iPhone
   notifications" > Choose iPhone… > scan, pick it, Save.
3. Allow the prompt on the iPhone if one appears. If it stays quiet:
   iPhone Settings > Bluetooth > (i) beside this PC > turn on
   Share System Notifications.

Honest expectations (please read this one)
- Bluetooth range only: it works while the phone is within roughly a
  room of the PC. Your messages still travel any distance through the
  Mac as always; this feature covers the phone-in-your-pocket case.
- It ships OFF and marked experimental. Every part I can prove in a
  test container is proven: the protocol layer is byte-exact under 18
  unit tests (fragmentation, unicode, malformed data, runaway buffers),
  and the whole in-app pipeline runs live in the harness (popups,
  sound, bell, bursts, dedupe, link transitions). The one thing no
  container can exercise is your actual Bluetooth adapter talking to
  your actual iPhone. If the link misbehaves there, it degrades to
  Activity-panel messages, never touches the rest of the app, and
  tools\phone_link_probe.py prints a step-by-step diagnostic you can
  send back.

Under the hood, for the record
- New app/phone package: ancs.py is the pure protocol (Notification
  Source events, Control Point commands, Data Source reassembly,
  filtering rules), link.py is the radio worker on its own thread with
  lazy imports, so machines without Bluetooth lose exactly one optional
  feature and nothing else. The background agent is untouched.
- The iPhone's pre-existing notification backlog is deliberately never
  alerted on connect (that would storm the screen every reconnection);
  only fresh, audible, newly posted notifications ring.
- 175 unit tests (25 new) and all four live harnesses pass, with every
  earlier regression guard unchanged.

To upgrade
Quit with the round power button, extract the zip over the old folder,
run install.bat once (it installs the Bluetooth support), confirm
Settings shows v3.3.0, then follow the three setup steps above.
