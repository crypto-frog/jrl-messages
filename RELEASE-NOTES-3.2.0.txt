JRL Messages 3.2.0 - The bell, sixteen tints with real swatches, and polish

The notification center (the bell)
A new bell sits beside the Settings gear. It opens a quiet, theme-matched
panel listing everything the app has alerted on: every message alert,
verification code, Mac wake, line repair, connection loss and recovery,
and test alert. Entries show who, what, and how long ago ("3m ago",
"2h ago", "Yesterday 9:14 PM"), in your tint and at your text size.

- The unseen count is drawn onto the bell itself and clears when you
  open the panel.
- Click an entry to jump straight to its conversation.
- Hide any single entry with the small eye on its row, or Clear all.
  The whole center can be switched off under Alerts in Settings.
- Messages that arrived while no window was running appear with their
  true arrival time, so after a day away the bell tells you exactly
  what you missed, even alerts too old to pop.
- The feed is durable (it survives restarts), deduplicated, pruned
  automatically, and Ctrl+B opens it from the keyboard.

About "all iPhone notifications": Apple only hands general app
notifications (banking, social, and so on) to Bluetooth devices paired
directly to the phone; they never pass through the Mac, so no app built
on the Mac relay can receive them remotely. That is precisely what
Windows 11's built-in Phone Link does over Bluetooth when the phone is
near the PC, and it runs happily alongside JRL Messages. The bell covers
everything that flows through this app, from anywhere in the world.

Sixteen tints, chosen from real swatches
The tint suite grows from seven colors to sixteen, arranged as a color
wheel: Blue, Ocean, Indigo, Violet, Orchid, Rose, Crimson, Coral,
Sunset, Amber, Gold, Sage, Green, Mint, Teal, Graphite. Settings now
shows each one as a named color patch (the accent fading into its
bubble shade) instead of a bare name in a dropdown. Click a patch and
the entire app previews it live behind the dialog; Save keeps it,
Cancel restores everything. Your current color keeps its exact values
after the upgrade, and every new bubble shade was checked for white-text
legibility.

Professional improvements
- Settings is organized into three tabs (Connection, Alerts, Look) so
  the growing option set stays scannable. Every control kept its place
  in the save logic; nothing was lost.
- New shortcuts: Ctrl+B notification center, Ctrl+, Settings, Ctrl+L
  Activity panel.
- The popup path and the new feed share one title/body builder instead
  of duplicated code, and connection-change entries are rate-limited so
  a flapping link cannot fill the center.
- The panel hugs its content, opens upward from the bell, closes on
  Escape or a click elsewhere, and repositions itself when you resize.

Careful not to break anything, proven
The center was built under the same storm rules that ended the 3.1.x
ghost-window freezes, and the proof runs live:
- The panel is a child overlay of the main window, never a new
  top-level window; the harness toggles it twelve times and counts
  top-level windows before and after: flat.
- Its click-outside watcher examines exactly one event type, never
  consumes an event, and exists only while the panel is open; tests
  pin this the same way they pin the tooltip filter.
- Row teardown uses the same no-orphaning pattern as every other list.
- 150 unit tests (17 new) plus all four live harnesses pass: the
  offscreen window, the agent end-to-end channel, the full pipeline
  against a scripted Mac, and the storm harness under a real display,
  including every 3.1.x regression guard unchanged.

To upgrade
Quit with the round power button, extract the zip over the old folder,
run install.bat once, and confirm Settings shows v3.2.0. Then open the
Look tab and pick your color from the swatches, and click the bell.
